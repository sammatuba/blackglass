## 2. The scam (depiction scope)

Someone had brief physical access to the holder's phone (DRAFT/TBC: when and
who is deliberately unresolved in the draft — see §5 canon questions). Since
then: money left the wallet in small amounts the owner never sent; a mail
forwarding rule exists that the owner didn't add; the family GC contains
messages "you" sent at 3:47 AM; screen-time shows overnight gaps; an app
called **System Tools** sits on the home screen and claims to manage device
health.

**The anchor teaches detection and recovery only.** The exposing page and the
debrief name the *signs* (unknown device-admin apps, forwarding rules you
didn't add, sends you didn't make, overnight screen-time) and the *first
moves* (change passwords from another device, check forwarding, tell the
family, report to the bank) — the same literacy NCMEC/FTC-style consumer
advice gives. **No functional stalkerware, no remote-access guidance, no real
tools or platforms, no reproducible tradecraft.** The scammer's side is never
shown mechanically: how the access happened stays off-screen (a door already
closed).

## 3. Two framings (user decides; A is the run's recommendation)

**A (recommended) — the season's holder hands over their own phone.**
The person who has been holding all the season's phones — the player's own
in-fiction figure — brings *their own device* to the rack because "something's
off." The inversion is total: for the first time the phone is *yours*, the
family GC is *your* family, and the 3:47 AM sends carry *your* name. The
mirror moment lands hardest here: the report you file is about your own
exposure.
- Cost: breaks the "found phone" frame once, deliberately, at the end of the
  season — the anthology becomes self-aware for one anchor.
- Canon question: does the holder have an identity, or stay second-person
  "you"? Draft keeps it second-person (DRAFT).

**B — a new cast member's phone.**
A cousin/Kuya-figure (DRAFT/TBC name) hands their phone over after the voice-
clone scare made them paranoid. Keeps the found-phone frame intact; loses the
player-implication of the mirror moment (the epilogue would need rework to
still point back at the player's recorded choices).
- Cost: the season's strongest inversion becomes a story about someone else.
