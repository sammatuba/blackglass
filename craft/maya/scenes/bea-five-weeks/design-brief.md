# Arc — "FIVE WEEKS" (Bea's algorithmic capture)

**Status:** design brief for the writers' room. Phase 2 — the project's first *single-POV, time-compressed* piece (a new structural form alongside the Rashomon mornings).
**Form:** one phone (Bea's), five frames = five weeks. Not refracted; a slow drift. The counterpoint to the acute scams: no single lie, just a feed quietly reshaping what feels normal.
**Read first:** `../../_meta/craft-guidelines.md` (esp. §3 consequence-is-drift, §4 ellipsis grammar, §2 choice-is-voice), `../../_meta/character-baseline.md` (Bea), the kangkong + voice-clone Bea drafts for her voice.

---

## 1. The premise in one paragraph

Bea works in digital communications at a platform-accountability NGO. She has given webinars on media literacy. She is the person who catches the bad link. Over five weeks, her own feed — the For You page, the engagement metrics, the quote-tweet economy — slowly entrains *her.* Not with lies: with rewards. The sharp dunk outperforms the careful thread, so the next thread is a little sharper. Nuance underperforms, so the hedges come off. The pile-on on someone "insufficiently pure" gets the numbers, so she joins it. By week five she is giving another media-literacy webinar — algorithmically captured while teaching others to see clearly. The "epistemic cowardice is choosing civility over clarity" tweet on her wallpaper, which once felt like courage, now reads as the alibi for who she has become.

The spine: **algorithmic acclimation is drift, not decision (§3). The expert is not immune — being able to name the trap is not the same as being outside it. No single step was a lie; the sum is a different person.**

---

## 2. The ellipsis grammar (§4) and the drift (§3)

- **Frame = week.** Each frame opens with a week header (Week 1 … Week 5) and a small dateline / metric. The reader learns the rhythm: between frames, weeks of scrolling we don't see.
- **Drift, not fork.** Choices are small and tonal (§2) — post the nuanced version or the punchy one; add the hedge or cut it; quote-tweet or scroll past. The *punchy / certain / tribal* option wins a little engagement each time, and the win is the hook. Accumulate quiet tags (no visible stat): `cuts_the_hedge`, `performs_certainty`, `mistakes_engagement_for_consensus`. The player should realise — at week five — they've become someone week-one Bea wouldn't recognise, without a number ever saying so.
- **The refusal:** Bea never admits, in the moment, that she's being shaped. Only the week-five frame lets the recognition land — and even then, gently, deniably.

## 3. The five weeks (seed; the writer shapes)

- **Week 1 — the hit.** A clean, sharp debunk-dunk of hers goes viral. The metrics. The particular dopamine of being *right, loudly, to applause.* First small choice: when the next post comes, do you keep the careful caveat, or cut it because the sharp version is what flew?
- **Week 2 — the trim.** The feed serves her more of what performed. She drafts a thread; the nuanced version is honest, the punchy version is shareable. She tells herself punchy *is* clarity. (It isn't, quite.)
- **Week 3 — the purity test.** Someone on "her side" — an ally, a fellow advocate — says something insufficiently pure. The quote-tweet pile-on is already forming and it has the numbers. Join it (it feels like accountability) or sit it out (and watch the engagement go to those who didn't)?
- **Week 4 — the hall of agreement.** She mutes / soft-blocks the accounts that complicate things. Her feed is calmer now, cleaner, all nodding. She mistakes the quiet for consensus — everyone agrees, so she must be right. (Everyone who disagreed is just muted.)
- **Week 5 — the webinar.** She gives a media-literacy talk: "how to spot manipulation, how to stay clear-eyed." Seventy people take notes. The slide says *do not trust a source just because it confirms you.* She believes it. She has also spent five weeks trusting the sources that confirmed her, and calling it literacy. The wallpaper tweet looks back at her. The recognition is available but never forced.

## 4. Output the writer should return

(1) a short phone-interior note (how Bea's feed/apps look as the weeks pass — the same home screen, subtly different in what it surfaces); (2) the polished arc, ~1000–1400 words across five week-frames, in the engine's beat vocabulary so it's buildable — `world`/`narr`/`aside`/`sys`/`msg`/`notif`/`video` and one `choice` per week (options with `set:{}` drift-tags + short `say` consequence beats; engagement-metric flavor welcome via `sys` or `notif`); each week-frame should carry a week header in its opening beat. (3) a 2–3 sentence editorial note on the drift mechanism and what it teaches. Online-fluent second person, em-dash interior, the council, Threads/TikTok-native. Treat Bea with dignity — her capture is recognizable and almost admirable from inside; she is not a cautionary cartoon.
